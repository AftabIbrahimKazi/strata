Reveal

Two stacked layers. The pointer opens a soft hole in the top one, showing what is underneath — only where the cursor is, not across the whole element. Move over anything below.

1 — Images

The classic case: a before and an after, revealed under the pointer. These are two inline SVGs rather than photographs, so the page stays self-contained — the preset does not care what the layers are. 

<div data-st-cfx-target="reveal">
  <img src="night.jpg" alt="…">              <!-- revealed underneath -->
  <img src="day.jpg" aria-hidden="true">     <!-- last child is masked -->
</div>

2 — Buttons

Two states stacked. The hole follows the pointer across the label, so the button reads as lit from underneath rather than simply swapping on hover.

The visible label sits underneath and the decorative layer on top carries aria-hidden="true" — otherwise a screen reader announces both.

3 — Text

Two type treatments of the same words. Both layers must carry identical text or the reveal tears as the hole moves.

5 — Retuned from CSS alone

Identical markup in all three. The only difference is a class setting --st-cfx-reveal-* — no attributes, no JavaScript. This is why an instance writes a property inline only when it overrides a default: anything left alone falls through to your stylesheet.

What the JS actually does

Two custom properties per pointer move, and one attribute flip on enter and leave. No render(), no per-frame work. The mask, its softness and both transitions are CSS. 

--st-cfx-reveal-x: 214.0px
--st-cfx-reveal-y: 96.0px
data-st-cfx-reveal="true"

Drag radius, then inspect a container: the property appears only once it differs from the default, and disappears again when you set it back.
